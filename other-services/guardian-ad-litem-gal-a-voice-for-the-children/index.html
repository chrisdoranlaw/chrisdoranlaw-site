Guardian Ad Litem (GAL): A Voice for the Children

In many family law cases, the adults are so focused on their own conflict that the needs of the children can get lost in the shuffle. This is where a Guardian Ad Litem, or GAL, comes in.

When I serve as a GAL, my client isn't the mom or the dad: it's the child. My responsibility is to investigate the situation and report back to the court about what is truly in the child's best interests. This might involve visiting the home, talking to teachers, and observing the child's interactions with both parents.

Why a GAL is Important

Being a Jennings County attorney means caring about the future of our community, and that starts with our kids. A GAL provides the judge with an objective view of the situation. I take this role very seriously because I know the impact these decisions have on a young person's life.

If you are involved in a high-conflict custody case, having a GAL can actually help de-escalate the situation. It moves the focus away from "who is right" and back to "what do the kids need."

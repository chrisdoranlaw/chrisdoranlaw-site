I'm in Indianapolis today for "Inhalants 101," an in-person session hosted by the Indiana State Department of Toxicology.

The presentation covers inhalants, including DFE and nitrous oxide, and how they affect the body and driving behavior. Melissa Galey, ISDT's forensic toxicologist, is leading it, with a Q&A afterward.

Why does that matter to a small-town practice in Jennings County? Because impaired driving cases increasingly involve substances that won't show up on a standard breath test. Understanding how these substances affect the body, and how the science gets tested, matters when the facts of a case are in dispute.

I wrote up the full details on the blog, including why a toxicology result isn't the same as proof of impairment: https://www.chrisdoranlaw.com/blog/inhalants-and-indiana-owi-cases-why-toxicology-matters

Every case turns on its own facts, and no two are the same. But staying current on the science behind these charges is part of doing this work well.
